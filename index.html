About

Agentic robotics brings perception, reasoning, and action into systems that pursue goals through interaction with the physical world.

Abstract

Advances in foundation models, robot learning, and world models create opportunities for robots to interpret instructions, plan over extended horizons, and adapt through feedback. This workshop brings together researchers in computer vision, robotics, and machine learning to examine the foundations and practice of agentic robotics.

Topics include grounded perception, planning, memory, policy learning, human and multi-robot collaboration, and evaluation. Through invited talks, contributed research, and discussion, we aim to identify shared challenges and opportunities for general-purpose embodied intelligence.

Our organizing theme is the relationship between individual capabilities and the behavior of the complete robotic system: how advances in visual understanding, planning, memory, policy learning, and interaction translate into effective action.

What we mean

We use agentic robotics to describe goal-directed robotic systems that select and revise their actions through repeated interaction with the physical world.

This covers modular, hierarchical, and end-to-end systems, connecting established work on perception, planning, control, and robot learning with emerging approaches based on foundation models, world models, and agent architectures.

Driving questions

The workshop is organized around five questions.

01

How should perception support goal-directed behavior?

What visual, spatial, and multimodal representations help robots understand objects, affordances, and changing environments?

02

How should reasoning and planning connect to action?

How can agents translate goals into executable behavior, coordinate high-level decisions with control, and revise plans using feedback?

03

How can robotic agents learn and adapt through experience?

What roles should demonstrations, reinforcement learning, memory, world models, and online interaction play in improving behavior?

04

How should robots collaborate with people and other agents?

How can communication, shared understanding, and human guidance support effective cooperation?

05

How should we evaluate and deploy agentic robotic systems?

Which tasks, metrics, and experimental protocols capture generalization, reliability, safety, and efficiency across extended interactions?
