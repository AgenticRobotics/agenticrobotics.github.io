About

Agentic robotics brings perception, reasoning, and action into systems that pursue goals through interaction with the physical world.

Abstract

Agentic robotics connects perception, reasoning, and action in systems that pursue goals through physical interaction. Advances in frontier multimodal models and robot learning make it timely to examine how these capabilities combine into reliable, efficient robotic agents. This workshop brings together computer vision, robotics, and machine learning researchers to discuss grounded perception and tool use, code-based and learned policies, planning, memory and world models, adaptation, collaboration, and evaluation. Through invited talks, contributed papers, posters, and a panel, we aim to identify shared challenges and research priorities for general-purpose embodied intelligence.

What we mean

We use agentic robotics to describe goal-directed robotic systems that select and revise their actions through repeated interaction with the physical world.

This covers modular, hierarchical, and end-to-end systems, connecting established work on perception, planning, control, and robot learning with emerging approaches based on foundation models, world models, and agent architectures.

Driving questions

The workshop is organized around five questions.

01

How should robotic agents perceive and acquire information for action?

How can visual and spatial understanding, active perception, and calls to detection, segmentation, or 3D perception tools provide the information needed to act?

02

How should reasoning, generated code, and planning connect to reliable execution?

How can code-as-policy, learned policies, and planners compose skills and APIs, check execution outcomes, and recover from failure?

03

How can robotic agents learn and adapt through experience?

What roles should demonstrations, reinforcement learning, memory, world models, and online interaction play in improving behavior?

04

How should robots collaborate with people and other agents?

How can communication, shared understanding, and human guidance support effective cooperation?

05

How should we evaluate and deploy reliable, efficient robotic agents?

Which protocols measure generalization and safety alongside inference latency, computation, and resource use? When is additional reasoning worth its execution cost?

Proposed submission details

  • Short papers: up to 4 pages of main text; long papers: up to 8 pages, excluding references.
  • Use the CVPR 2027 paper template. Video and code supplements are encouraged.
  • Non-archival: accepted work will be shared through OpenReview and this website with author permission, without proceedings publication.
  • Double-blind review, with three independent reviews per paper.
  • Accepted papers receive posters; four will be selected for contributed talks.
  • Submission portal: OpenReview link to be announced.
  • No standalone competition is planned.

Participation and support

We welcome participation across career stages, research communities, institutions, and regions.

Diversity and participation grants

Our organizing team includes Ph.D. students, postdoctoral researchers, faculty, and industry researchers at institutions in the United States, the United Kingdom, and Singapore. Our speakers bring academic and industrial perspectives on visual understanding, robot learning, planning, control, and multiagent systems.

We have secured USD 8,000 in sponsorship to support registration and travel grants for students, early-career researchers, and participants with limited institutional funding. Awards will prioritize financial need and participation barriers. Application instructions, available amounts, and selection criteria will be announced here.

Mentoring, accessibility, and materials

We will pair junior reviewers with experienced program committee members for constructive review guidance and feedback. Targeted outreach will encourage participation from institutions and communities with fewer opportunities to attend major vision and robotics meetings.

We will collect audience questions in advance and encourage accessible slides and author-approved presentation materials. Recordings and any prerecorded alternatives will follow CVPR's arrangements and speaker permissions. This website will link accepted papers, approved materials, and a synthesis of the panel's open questions and research priorities.
